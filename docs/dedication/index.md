_I have been thinking a lot about passing on tools and techniques, and how to ensure that these remain deeply connected with and centered with living Korean people and communities, especially here in the diaspora. Hanji craft, or any other ancestral craft, is so much more than just its materials and tools and techniques. It’s a vital heritage, a living connection with our people, our ancestors, our memories, and our land, in personal, multisensory, spiritual, ecological, cultural, and political ways._

_What I really appreciate is when folks and institutions who aren’t Korean understand that this work goes much deeper than academic or historical study for us, and how special it is for diasporic Koreans to be able to come together to rebuild these practices together. That the social practice of these crafts, the stitching back together of our communities through them, is what keeps them alive. The centering of Korean people and community in these craft practices reflects the understanding that they are a part of our work to sustain and steward ways of being that connect us with our ancestors going back millenia._ 

## Dedication of Korean ancestral tools and techniques

This work is dedicated to fellow Korean diaspora craft practitioners, as part of our generations-long effort to sustain our relationships not only with the worlds of our ancestors through the materials and techniques of these crafts, but with the spirit these practices embody, the ecological relationships they play a part in, and the liberative worlds we hope to co-create together.
